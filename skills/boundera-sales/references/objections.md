# Objection Patterns

Use these as patterns, not scripts. Keep replies short and specific to the thread.

## Timing / Not Now

Signal:
- "Not a good time"
- "Could change down the road"
- "We are not ready yet"

Response posture:
- Agree timing matters.
- Preserve relationship.
- Name a future trigger.
- Do not ask for another meeting immediately.

Template:
> Totally understand. Sounds like timing is the issue, not necessarily the category.
>
> I will keep you lightly in the loop as the sandbox and FedRAMP 20x evidence workflows mature. If a client situation comes up where evidence review, automation, or continuous monitoring becomes painful, happy to reconnect.

## Not My Role

Signal:
- "I do not do sales/business development"
- "I am delivery only"
- "I do not cover that area"

Response posture:
- Do not force the original motion.
- Reframe to their world if relevant.
- Ask for routing.

Template:
> Totally fair. I was trying to understand where this fits internally.
>
> Who usually owns conversations around FedRAMP tooling or partner solutions for vendors/customers?

## Send Me Info / Email Me

Signal:
- "Send me a note"
- "Email me"
- "Send details"

Response posture:
- Confirm.
- Move to requested channel.
- Do not keep pushing on LinkedIn unless they engage there later.

Template:
> Got it. I will send a short note there.

If following up later:
> Quick follow-up by email only, since you asked me to route it there. I sent the overview and wanted to see if there is a better person for FedRAMP partner/vendor fit.

## Confusion

Signal:
- "I do not understand your question"
- Their reply indicates the ask was too abstract.

Response posture:
- Simplify.
- Remove jargon.
- Make the next ask concrete.

Template:
> Fair point. Simpler version: we help vendors working through FedRAMP reduce manual evidence, POA&M, and authorization package work.
>
> Is there a team that evaluates tools like this for your vendor ecosystem?

## Explicit Channel Boundary

Signal:
- "Stop contacting me here"
- "Use email"
- "Send all requests to email"

Response posture:
- Acknowledge once.
- Stop.
- Do not draft another LinkedIn message.

Template:
> Understood. I will keep this to email going forward.

## Polite No / Not a Fit

Signal:
- "Not a fit"
- "We do not need this"
- "No interest"

Response posture:
- Do not argue.
- Leave cleanly unless they invite future contact.

Template:
> Understood. Appreciate you taking a look.

If they invite future contact:
> Appreciate you taking a look. I will stay out of your way for now and reconnect only if something materially relevant changes.

## Positive But Vague

Signal:
- "Interesting"
- Emoji/reaction
- Compliment without clear next step

Response posture:
- Do not jump straight to a full meeting ask.
- Ask for expert feedback or routing.

Template:
> Appreciate that. Given your background, I would value your blunt take: does this map to a real FedRAMP workflow pain, or does the framing miss how teams actually evaluate this?

## Forwarded Internally

Signal:
- "I forwarded it"
- "I passed it along"

Response posture:
- Thank them.
- Do not ask them to route again.
- Wait for inbound or follow up by the channel they allowed.

Template:
> Appreciate you forwarding it. I will wait to hear from the right person and keep future follow-up to email.
