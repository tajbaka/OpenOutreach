# Boundera Sales Motion Summary

This is the canonical conversation-level reference for Boundera's 15-step sales motion, adapted from [the source sales-process video](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=YS9In813jJ0). Use it for account strategy, call preparation, meeting notes, and next-step recommendations. The separate Sales Motion Google Sheet remains the account-specific tracker.

The source describes 14 numbered steps while calling the process closer to 15. This reference treats expansion immediately after signature as step 15.

## How to use this reference

1. Identify the opportunity's current step from actual evidence; do not infer progress from a positive tone.
2. Ask only the questions needed for the current meeting. Recover missed discovery naturally instead of turning a demo into an interrogation.
3. Separate process tasks from operating guidance. A task has a completion state; guidance describes how to execute it well.
4. Record customer language, named people, commitments, dates, and unresolved questions. Mark uncertain facts as unknown.
5. End every substantive interaction with a concrete next step, owner, and date.
6. Do not pull pricing, procurement, approval, or signature questions forward before the customer has experienced enough value to answer them meaningfully.

## Stakeholder roles are not interchangeable

- **Champion:** wants the project to succeed and helps navigate the account.
- **End user:** will work in the product or workflow.
- **Evaluator or technical stakeholder:** determines whether the product meets operational, security, or technical requirements.
- **Executive sponsor or economic buyer:** supports the initiative and controls or influences budget.
- **Approver:** must approve the purchase, security review, procurement path, or budget.
- **Authorized signatory:** is legally permitted to sign the agreement; this may not be the sponsor or approver.

Avoid recording someone as a generic "decision-maker" when the precise role is still unknown.

## The 15 steps

### 1. Land the first meeting

**Outcome:** A relevant executive or direct report accepts an introductory conversation.

- Target the executive and/or N-minus-one with a coordinated founder and sales approach.
- Experiment across email, LinkedIn, calls, events, warm introductions, and other appropriate channels.
- Keep outreach to two or three sentences and sell the strategic advantage, not a feature inventory.
- Refine the message from actual response patterns.

### 2. Run the introductory call

**Outcome:** Understand the buyer's situation well enough to decide whether and how to continue.

- Learn priorities, organizational dynamics, current approach, desired change, decision criteria, urgency, and the cost of doing nothing.
- Distinguish executive value from end-user value.
- Capture account-specific information competitors probably do not know.
- Keep the call informal and buyer-led. Do not use slides, force a canned qualification script, or give a full product demo.
- Bring the founder when a senior executive attends.

### 3. Conduct the pre-demo follow-up

**Outcome:** Co-author the group demo with the emerging champion.

- Hold a short 15–30 minute champion call.
- Confirm what was heard, choose the product areas that will resonate, and decide who must attend.
- Learn what each attendee cares about, what to avoid, and whether anyone needs a private preview.
- Give the champion visible input and book the group demo together.

### 4. Frame and prepare the demo

**Outcome:** Enter the demo with a buyer-specific narrative and definition of success.

- Research every participant and their role.
- Map the session to the buyer's language, priorities, and desired change.
- Define what a successful session looks like and the right balance of discussion versus product.
- Prepare context for new stakeholders, give the champion ownership, and select only the most relevant product workflow.

### 5. Run the group demo

**Outcome:** The deciding, using, sponsoring, and implementing stakeholders see the relevant value in their own context.

- Begin with the customer's situation and desired change, not Boundera's company history.
- Ask new participants what they want from the session.
- Show the 20% of the product that provides 80% of the relevant value.
- Let useful questions expand the discussion without losing the core narrative.
- Do not demonstrate every feature; irrelevant capability creates distraction and objections.

### 6. Debrief immediately after the demo

**Outcome:** Obtain a candid read on the group and decide whether the opportunity should advance.

- Contact the champion within roughly five minutes when possible.
- Ask for the unfiltered reaction before internal opinions harden.
- Identify who was excited, skeptical, confused, disengaged, or likely to block progress.
- Arrange targeted conversations with uncertain stakeholders and agree on the next step.

### 7. Define the pilot's path forward

**Outcome:** A pilot has an owner, real users, a bounded structure, and an agreed commercial destination.

- Identify the internal pilot owner and three or four actual users.
- Decide whether value can be proven without customer data, integration, or customization.
- Set duration and commercial structure according to the work required.
- Confirm what should happen commercially if the pilot succeeds.

### 8. Prepare the pilot

**Outcome:** The customer and Boundera agree on success, tasks, measurement, and the path to purchase.

- Co-author precise success criteria and give participants two or three specific tasks.
- Schedule onboarding and define how results will be measured.
- Work backward from the target signature date.
- Identify procurement, legal, security, IT, finance, and purchase-approval requirements.
- Discuss pricing after the demo, when the stakeholders understand the value.
- Delay the pilot if the organization cannot act soon after it ends.

### 9. Run the pilot

**Outcome:** The pilot generates reliable evidence of business and user value.

- Time-box and actively project-manage the pilot.
- Ensure every participant knows what to test.
- Monitor usage, task completion, bugs, confusion, and inconsistent experiences.
- Use the champion to re-engage inactive participants and resolve problems immediately.
- Do not give unrestricted access and wait for users to discover value themselves.

### 10. Hold the post-pilot session

**Outcome:** The group makes an evidence-backed decision about proceeding.

- Collect feedback and compare actual usage with the agreed success criteria.
- Ask what worked, what did not, and why.
- Address objections directly and distinguish product gaps from poor onboarding or low participation.
- Confirm whether the group wants to proceed.
- Treat sudden champion silence as a warning signal, not neutral behavior.

### 11. Prepare the paperwork and procurement timeline

**Outcome:** The champion has a forwardable commercial summary and procurement has a viable path to the target date.

- Summarize pricing, scope, timing, and commercial terms in email.
- Establish a legitimate target completion date.
- Send an editable Microsoft Word contract and ask whether procurement prefers its own template.
- Introduce procurement early enough to meet the signature target.
- Do not begin implementation before the agreement is signed.

### 12. Review the contract and redlines

**Outcome:** Low-impact edits move quickly and material disagreements are isolated.

- Accept straightforward changes quickly and focus negotiation on provisions that materially affect the business.
- Use qualified legal counsel.
- Move extensive redlines to a live legal call once the parties are mostly aligned.

### 13. Work through procurement and legal

**Outcome:** Both sides resolve the remaining material terms without losing deal momentum.

- Bring procurement and legal leads into a live discussion.
- Resolve the provisions neither side can accept.
- Push back on disproportionate requirements and explain Boundera's real constraints and exposure.
- Treat procurement as a buying-process stakeholder, not an automatic deal blocker.

### 14. Route and obtain the signature

**Outcome:** Every required signature is complete.

- Identify the authorized signatory before routing the agreement.
- Do not assume the sponsor or approver has signing authority.
- Confirm the routing path and expected completion date with the champion.
- Track the agreement and ask the champion to nudge the signatory when needed.
- Keep the opportunity open until all required signatures are complete.

### 15. Begin expansion planning

**Outcome:** The initial agreement creates an explicit path to broader value.

- Start the expansion conversation as soon as the first agreement is signed.
- Identify additional teams, use cases, products, seats, or business units.
- Establish an initial baseline and the outcomes that would justify a larger commitment.
- Consider a two-year structure when year-two expansion has credible value.

## Question timing guide

Use the least aggressive question that gets the information required at the current stage.

| Timing | Purpose | Example |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Introductory call | Understand operational ownership | “How are you dividing ownership of this work today?” |
| Introductory call | Understand urgency | “What made this worth evaluating now?” |
| Pre-demo or opening | Define session value | “What are the main one or two things you want to validate today?” |
| End of demo | Discover the next stakeholder without forcing qualification | “If this works the way you need, what would the natural next step be—and who else would you want involved?” |
| Pilot planning | Identify purchase approval | “If the pilot is successful, who would need to approve moving forward?” |
| Contract routing | Identify the legal signer | “Who is authorized to sign the agreement, and how does it get routed to them?” |

Do not ask “Which executive signs off before you use us?” during an early demo. It assumes the buyer, process, outcome, and signing role before they have been established.
