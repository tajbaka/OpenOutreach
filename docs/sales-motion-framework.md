# Boundera Sales Motion — Detailed Framework

This is the complete conversation-level reconstruction behind the canonical Sales Motion Google Sheet. It preserves all 85 stable task IDs (`1a` through `15e`) and keeps process tasks separate from operating guidance.

The live `Template` tab remains authoritative for the tracker wording and native Sheet structure. This document mirrors that wording so call preparation, account analysis, and future automation can use the framework without flattening the Sheet.

Source episode: [How to close $100K+ enterprise deals, step by step | Jen Abel](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=YS9In813jJ0). The conversation explicitly numbers 14 steps while describing the process as closer to 15. Because expansion begins immediately after signature, Boundera treats expansion as the practical fifteenth step.

For the concise operating reference and question-timing rules, read [sales-motion-summary.md](sales-motion-summary.md). For timestamped source notes, read [sales-motion-video-transcript.md](sales-motion-video-transcript.md).

## 1. Land the first meeting

### Process checklist

- **1a.** Target the executive decision-maker or their direct report ("N-minus-one").
- **1b.** Use a pincer approach: the founder contacts the executive while the sales lead contacts the N-minus-one.
- **1c.** Experiment with email, LinkedIn, calls, events, and warm introductions.

### Operating guidance

- Keep outreach to two or three sentences.
- Sell the "alpha"—the strategic advantage or new opportunity—not merely a feature or efficiency gain.
- Explain what the executive could accomplish or become known for by adopting the solution.
- Continually refine the message based on response rates.

## 2. Run the introductory call

### Process checklist

- **2a.** Ask about priorities, organizational dynamics, existing approaches, desired change, and decision criteria.
- **2b.** Understand why the issue matters now and what happens if nothing changes.
- **2c.** Identify the executive value as well as the end-user value.
- **2d.** Capture account-specific information that competitors probably do not have.
- **2e.** Bring the founder if a senior executive attends.

### Operating guidance

- Treat the introductory call as the most important intelligence-gathering conversation.
- Keep it informal, conversational, and focused on the buyer.
- Do not show slides or give a product demo.
- Listen for information that competitors probably do not have.
- Avoid forcing the buyer through a canned qualification script.

## 3. Conduct the pre-demo follow-up

### Process checklist

- **3a.** Schedule a short 15–30 minute call with the emerging internal champion.
- **3b.** Confirm what you heard during the introductory call.
- **3c.** Ask which product areas will resonate most.
- **3d.** Determine who needs to attend the full demo.
- **3e.** Ask what each participant cares about and what should be avoided.
- **3f.** Decide whether another stakeholder needs a private pre-demo first.
- **3g.** Invite the champion to shape the demo and suggest questions.
- **3h.** Book the group demo while you are together.

### Operating guidance

- Use the pre-demo interaction to co-author the demo with the champion rather than treating it as another generic sales meeting.

## 4. Frame and prepare the demo

### Process checklist

- **4a.** Research every person attending and their role in the decision.
- **4b.** Map the pitch directly to the buyer's language and priorities.
- **4c.** Define what a successful demo looks like for this particular organization.
- **4d.** Determine the right balance of conversation and product demonstration.
- **4e.** Prepare a tailored opening for stakeholders who missed the earlier calls.
- **4f.** Give the champion visible ownership so the purchase feels internally created.
- **4g.** Plan to demonstrate only the most relevant part of the product.

### Operating guidance

- Optimize for buyer specificity rather than product completeness.
- Make the demo feel internally co-authored, not imposed by Boundera.

## 5. Run the group demo

### Process checklist

- **5a.** Include the people responsible for deciding, using, sponsoring, and implementing the product.
- **5b.** Begin with the customer's situation and desired change—not your company history.
- **5c.** Ask new participants what they want from the session.
- **5d.** Demonstrate the 20% of the product that delivers 80% of the relevant value.
- **5e.** Let stakeholder questions guide expansion into other features.

### Operating guidance

- Make the product feel specifically designed for their organization.
- Do not demonstrate every capability; irrelevant features can dilute value or create objections.
- Maintain a tight narrative while allowing useful discussion.

## 6. Debrief immediately after the demo

### Process checklist

- **6a.** Contact the champion within roughly five minutes.
- **6b.** Ask for their unfiltered reaction before internal opinions solidify.
- **6c.** Find out who was excited, confused, skeptical, or disengaged.
- **6d.** Ask where the presentation landed well and where it lost people.
- **6e.** Identify the person most likely to block the deal.
- **6f.** Arrange targeted follow-up conversations with uncertain stakeholders.
- **6g.** Use the feedback to determine whether the opportunity should advance.

### Operating guidance

- Seek an unfiltered reaction, not polite affirmation.
- Look actively for the likely blocker while reactions are still fresh.

## 7. Define the pilot's path forward

### Process checklist

- **7a.** Identify who owns the pilot internally.
- **7b.** Select three or four actual users; do not expect the executive sponsor to use the product.
- **7c.** Decide whether the product can prove value without customer data or integration.
- **7d.** Choose the pilot duration and commercial structure based on the integration work required.
- **7e.** Confirm what happens commercially if the pilot succeeds.

### Operating guidance

- Prefer a short pilot when it can prove value without artificial integration work.
- Charge for longer pilots that require material integration or customization.
- Consider crediting a paid pilot fee toward the final contract.

## 8. Prepare the pilot

### Process checklist

- **8a.** Co-author a precise definition of success with the customer.
- **8b.** Give participants two or three specific tasks rather than unrestricted access.
- **8c.** Schedule individual or small-group onboarding sessions.
- **8d.** Establish how results will be measured and collected.
- **8e.** Work backward from the desired signature date.
- **8f.** Identify procurement, legal, security, IT, and finance requirements.
- **8g.** Confirm who must approve the purchase.
- **8h.** Discuss pricing after the demo, once stakeholders understand the value.

### Operating guidance

- Delay the pilot if the organization cannot act soon afterward; otherwise momentum will disappear.

## 9. Run the pilot

### Process checklist

- **9a.** Time-box it and actively project-manage the experience.
- **9b.** Ensure every participant knows exactly what to test.
- **9c.** Monitor engagement, usage, completion, bugs, and confusion.
- **9d.** Use the champion to follow up with participants who are not engaging.
- **9e.** Keep different users' experiences reasonably consistent.
- **9f.** Resolve problems immediately rather than waiting for the final review.
- **9g.** Capture concrete evidence of business and user value.

### Operating guidance

- Do not let users simply log in and "meander."

## 10. Hold the post-pilot session

### Process checklist

- **10a.** Collect feedback through interviews, surveys, product data, or the champion.
- **10b.** Compare actual usage with the agreed success criteria.
- **10c.** Ask what worked, what did not, and why.
- **10d.** Address objections directly rather than accepting polite, vague feedback.
- **10e.** Distinguish product shortcomings from poor onboarding or low participation.
- **10f.** Confirm whether the group wants to proceed.

### Operating guidance

- Use an approximately 80% post-pilot advancement rate as a diagnostic benchmark for properly qualified pilots, not as a guarantee.
- Treat sudden silence from the champion as a signal that the deal may be weakening.

## 11. Prepare the paperwork and procurement timeline

### Process checklist

- **11a.** Document the agreed pricing, scope, timeline, and commercial terms in an email.
- **11b.** Give the champion something clear to forward internally.
- **11c.** Create a legitimate reason to complete the agreement by a specific date.
- **11d.** Send an editable Microsoft Word contract—not a PDF or Google Doc.
- **11e.** Ask whether procurement prefers our contract or its own template.
- **11f.** Introduce procurement early enough to meet the target signature date.

### Operating guidance

- Do not begin implementation before procurement is complete and the agreement is signed.

## 12. Review the contract and redlines

### Process checklist

- **12a.** Accept straightforward, low-impact changes quickly.
- **12b.** Focus negotiation on provisions that materially affect the business.
- **12c.** Have qualified legal counsel review the proposed changes.
- **12d.** Request a live legal call once the parties are mostly aligned.

### Operating guidance

- Expect the customer to return redlines.
- Avoid prolonged email exchanges when extensive redlines appear.
- Pick our battles rather than treating every clause as equally important.

## 13. Work through procurement and legal

### Process checklist

- **13a.** Bring the customer's procurement and legal leads into a live discussion.
- **13b.** Resolve the provisions neither party can accept.
- **13c.** Push back on disproportionate requirements such as excessive insurance or liability.
- **13d.** Explain Boundera's practical constraints and actual exposure.

### Operating guidance

- Expect the customer's template to contain broad, buyer-friendly terms.
- Remember that many terms are negotiable at this stage.
- Do not assume procurement is trying to kill the deal; its role is to make the purchase compliant with company policy.

## 14. Route and obtain the signature

### Process checklist

- **14a.** Identify the authorized signatory before sending the contract.
- **14b.** Do not assume the executive sponsor has signing authority; it may belong to the CFO or another executive.
- **14c.** Make sure the internal champion knows who must sign.
- **14d.** Track the routing process and expected completion date.
- **14e.** Ask the champion to nudge the signatory if the agreement stalls.

### Operating guidance

- Treat the deal as open until every required signature is complete.

## 15. Begin expansion planning

### Process checklist

- **15a.** Start the expansion conversation as soon as the initial agreement is signed.
- **15b.** Identify additional teams, use cases, products, seats, or business units.
- **15c.** Establish a baseline for the initial deployment.
- **15d.** Define the outcomes that would justify a larger second-year commitment.
- **15e.** Consider a two-year agreement that introduces greater value or scope in year two.

### Operating guidance

- Use the information and relationships earned during the first sale to navigate the organization.
- Treat the first contract as the "land"; the larger commercial opportunity comes through expansion.
